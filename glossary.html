The design
glossary.

A working dictionary of the words behind the work — design, UI/UX, product and arts. No textbook recitals; just the terms I actually reach for, defined the way I’d explain them across a desk. Search it, filter it, follow the threads.

50 / 50 terms
A

001Accessibilitya11y

UI/UX

Designing so the widest possible range of people can perceive, operate and understand an interface — regardless of vision, motor, hearing or cognitive ability. Not a feature you bolt on; a constraint you design within from the first sketch.

See also

002Affordance

UI/UX

The perceived properties of an element that suggest how it can be used — a button looks pressable, a slider draggable, a card tappable. The gap between what something can do and what it looks like it can do is where confusion lives.

See also

003Atomic Design

Design

A methodology that builds interfaces from the smallest parts up: atoms → molecules → organisms → templates → pages. It gives a shared mental shape to a system so designers and engineers name the same things the same way.

See also
B

004Baseline Grid

Design

A horizontal rhythm that snaps text and elements to consistent vertical increments, so type sitting in different columns shares the same invisible footing. It's what makes a dense layout feel calm instead of jittery.

See also

005Bauhaus

Arts

The early-20th-century German school that fused craft with industry and still underwrites modern design — form follows function, geometry over ornament, primary colour, honest materials. A hundred years on, the loudest lesson is restraint.

See also
C

006Card Sorting

Product

A research method where users group labelled cards into categories that make sense to them — revealing how people expect content to be organised, rather than how the org chart happens to be drawn.

See also

007Component

Design

A reusable, self-contained interface element with defined states and properties — the working unit of a design system. Build it once, get it right once, and every screen inherits the fix.

See also

008Contrast Ratio

UI/UX

The measurable difference in luminance between foreground and background. WCAG asks for at least 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text — the line between “looks nice on my monitor” and “readable on a phone in sunlight.”

See also
D

009Design System

Design

A single source of truth — tokens, components, patterns and the guidelines that govern them — that keeps a product coherent as teams and surfaces multiply. Less a library, more a shared language with rules of grammar.

See also

010Design Token

Design

A named, platform-agnostic value — a colour, a spacing step, a radius — that carries a design decision into code without redrawing it. Change the token, and every screen referencing it updates at once.

See also

011Divergent Thinking

Design

Deliberately generating many options before narrowing to one — the wide half of any honest design process. Skip it and you don't choose the best idea, you choose the first one.

See also

012Double Diamond

Product

A process model in four moves — discover, define, develop, deliver — that alternates divergent and convergent thinking. The first diamond finds the right problem; the second finds the right solution.

See also
E

013Empty State

UI/UX

The screen shown when there's no content yet — the first impression nobody demos. Treated well it onboards and points the way; treated as an afterthought it reads as a dead end.

See also
F

014Figure–Ground

Arts

The visual relationship that lets the eye separate a subject from its surroundings. When it's ambiguous the composition feels unstable; when it's clear, the focal point reads instantly.

See also

015Fitts’s Law

UI/UX

The time to acquire a target is a function of its size and distance — bigger, closer targets are faster to hit. It's why primary actions are large and corners (infinitely deep) are prime real estate.

See also

016Flow

Product

The state of focused immersion that arrives when challenge meets skill. Good products protect it — they remove friction and resist interrupting; they don't manufacture reasons to pull you back in.

See also
G

017Gestalt Principles

Design

Rules of perception — proximity, similarity, closure, continuity — describing how the mind groups separate parts into wholes. Most layout decisions are just these principles applied on purpose instead of by accident.

See also

018Golden Ratio

Arts

A proportion of roughly 1:1.618 that recurs in nature and classical composition. A useful starting proportion — not a magic one; a balanced layout owes more to intent than to any single number.

See also

019Grid

Design

The invisible scaffolding of columns, gutters and margins that brings order and alignment to a layout. Constraints that make decisions faster and a composition feel deliberate rather than arranged by hand.

See also
H

020Heuristic Evaluation

UI/UX

A fast expert review of an interface against established usability principles. Cheaper and quicker than testing — it catches the obvious problems so user sessions can spend their time on the subtle ones.

See also

021Hick’s Law

UI/UX

The more choices you present, the longer the decision takes. The argument for fewer top-level options, sensible defaults, and revealing complexity only when it's asked for.

See also

022Hierarchy

Design

The intentional ordering of elements by importance so the eye knows where to look first, second, third. Built from size, weight, colour and space — when everything shouts, nothing is heard.

See also
I

023Information ArchitectureIA

Product

The structure and labelling of content so people can find what they need and always know where they are. Invisible when it works; the source of most “I can't find anything” complaints when it doesn't.

See also

024Iteration

Product

Improving a design through repeated cycles of build, test and refine. The opposite of getting it perfect on paper — you ship something honest, learn from contact with reality, and go again.

See also
J

025Jobs to Be DoneJTBD

Product

A lens that frames a product around the progress a person is trying to make — “hire” the product to do a job — rather than around demographics or features. People don't want a drill; they want a hole.

See also
K

026Kanban

Product

A visual workflow board that moves work across columns and limits work-in-progress so bottlenecks surface instead of hiding. Borrowed from a factory floor; at home on any team that values finishing over starting.

See also

027Kerning

Design

Adjusting the space between specific letter pairs so a word reads evenly — the “AV” and “To” that look gappy until you close them up. Per-pair surgery, not the uniform spacing of tracking.

See also
L

028Leading

Design

The vertical space between lines of type — line-height, named for the strips of lead once set between rows of metal type. Too tight and lines collide; too loose and they drift apart into unrelated bands.

See also
M

029Mental Model

UI/UX

The internal picture a user holds of how a system works. Friction appears precisely where that picture and the actual design disagree — so the cheapest usability win is often matching expectations, not teaching new ones.

See also

030Microcopy

UI/UX

The small functional words — button labels, hints, empty states, error messages — that quietly guide and reassure. A few words at the right moment do the work of an entire help page.

See also

031Modular Scale

Design

A harmonious sequence of sizes derived from a ratio, used to set type and spacing so steps relate to one another musically instead of arbitrarily. The reason a well-tuned layout feels “in key.”

See also

032MVP

Product

Minimum Viable Product — the smallest build that delivers real value and tests a core assumption with actual users. Minimum and viable carry equal weight; ship too thin and you've only learned that nobody likes a broken thing.

See also
N

033Negative Space

Arts

The empty area around and between subjects — active, shaped, intentional, never just leftover. Master it and the space starts doing as much work as the thing it surrounds.

See also
O

034Onboarding

Product

The first-run experience that carries a new user from arrival to their first moment of real value — the “aha.” Less a tour to sit through, more the shortest honest path to something that works.

See also
P

035Persona

Product

A composite character built from research that keeps a team designing for real people, not themselves. Useful as a tie-breaker — “would Priya the warehouse lead actually tap this?” — dangerous when invented from thin air.

See also

036Progressive Disclosure

UI/UX

Revealing complexity only as it's needed, keeping the default view simple while the power stays one step away. The cure for interfaces that frighten newcomers and the alternative to dumbing things down.

See also

037Prototype

Design

A testable mock — paper, clickable or coded — used to learn before committing to build. Its job isn't to look finished; it's to answer a specific question as cheaply as possible.

See also

038Proximity

Design

The Gestalt principle that elements placed near each other read as related. Often the fastest fix for a confusing layout is spacing alone: group what belongs together, separate what doesn't.

See also
R

039Responsive Design

UI/UX

Layouts that adapt fluidly across screen sizes, inputs and contexts — one design that reflows rather than a handful of fixed ones. Designing for the unknown device as much as the one on your desk.

See also

040Rule of Thirds

Arts

A composition guide that places focal points along the lines and intersections of a 3×3 grid, off-centre, for tension and balance. A reliable default — and, like all rules, most useful once you know when to break it.

See also
S

041Signifier

UI/UX

A perceivable cue that communicates an affordance — the underline that says “link,” the handle that says “drag me.” Affordances are possibilities; signifiers are how you announce them.

See also

042Skeuomorphism

UI/UX

Mimicking real-world materials and textures — stitched leather, paper, felt — so digital objects feel familiar. A bridge for new users that can curdle into decoration once the metaphor has done its teaching.

See also
T

043Tone of Voice

Product

The consistent personality of a product's language across every screen, email and error. The difference between software that sounds like a form and software that sounds like someone who's on your side.

See also

044Tracking

Design

Uniform letter-spacing across a whole run of text, distinct from per-pair kerning. Loosened a touch, small caps and all-caps labels breathe; over-tracked body copy just falls apart.

See also
U

045Usability Testing

UI/UX

Watching real users attempt real tasks to find where a design breaks down — not asking whether they like it, but seeing whether they can do it. Five people will surface most of what's wrong.

See also

046User Story

Product

A short, user-framed statement of need: “As a __, I want __, so that __.” It keeps a backlog item anchored to a human goal instead of drifting into a spec for its own sake.

See also
V

047Vertical Rhythm

Design

Consistent spacing that gives a layout an even, predictable cadence down the page — built on the baseline grid and the spacing scale. The quiet reason a page feels composed rather than assembled.

See also
W

048WCAG

UI/UX

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — the international standard for measurable accessible design, organised around four ideas: perceivable, operable, understandable, robust. The yardstick behind most accessibility requirements.

See also

049White Space

Arts

The breathing room in a composition — restraint that gives content focus. Rarely the first thing a stakeholder wants to pay for, almost always the thing that makes the work look expensive.

See also

050Wireframe

Design

A low-fidelity skeleton that settles structure and priority before colour, type or polish enter the room. Deliberately ugly, so the conversation stays about what goes where and why.
